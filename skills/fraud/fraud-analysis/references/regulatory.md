# Escalation and regulatory reference

This is a US-oriented summary to guide the analyst. The bank's policy team
owns the authoritative rules.

## Escalate to financial crimes when
- the exposure is above $5,000, or several accounts are involved;
- the payee looks like a money mule (new account, many unrelated senders);
- the pattern looks organised or repeats (same device, IP or payee across
  customers);
- an insider or employee may be involved;
- the case involves elder or vulnerable-customer abuse;
- the activity may require a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR).

## Considerations to list when they apply
- **Regulation E (electronic fund transfers, debit cards, P2P):** the
  customer's liability depends on how quickly they report it (2 business
  days, or 60 days after the statement). Provisional credit is usually due
  within 10 business days while the investigation runs.
- **Regulation Z (credit cards):** the customer's liability for unauthorized
  use is capped at $50, and most issuers waive it. Billing-error disputes must
  be raised within 60 days of the statement.
- **Card network chargeback windows:** typically 120 days from the
  transaction. File early.
- **SAR filing:** within 30 days of first detecting the activity, when
  thresholds are met.
- **Authorized push payment scams:** may fall outside Reg E protection, so
  note the reimbursement policy that applies.
- **Vulnerable customers:** follow the elder-abuse reporting duties and the
  internal vulnerable-customer policy.
- **Data breach:** if the customer's credentials or data were exposed through
  the bank's channels, notify cyber security and privacy.
